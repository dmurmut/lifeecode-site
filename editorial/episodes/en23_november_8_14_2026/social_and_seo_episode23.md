## LinkedIn

When a health company says "private," what exactly does it promise?

This week, the FTC and seven states amended their lawsuit against telehealth company Hims & Hers. According to the complaint, the company promised that only the providers managing your care could access your medical records and sensitive information — yet allegedly uploaded about 14 million customer email addresses to Meta for ad audiences and sent sign-up and purchase events tagged by treatment category, including mental health.

These are allegations; no court has ruled, and Hims & Hers says it will vigorously defend itself. But the case highlights a gap many organisations underestimate: protecting clinical records is not the same as protecting the fact that someone sought a particular treatment.

For health-tech leaders, three questions worth asking this week:
• Which tracking tools run on sign-up and checkout pages?
• Do event names or audience names reveal a treatment category?
• Do marketing privacy claims match what the data flows actually do?
