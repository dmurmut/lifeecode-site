## LinkedIn

The new HIPAA Privacy Rule is a real win for patient access: 15 days to respond to a records request (down from 30), posted fee schedules, and a clearer duty to deliver records through a personal health app of the patient's choosing.

It also sharpens a gap we don't talk about enough. HIPAA follows the holder, not the data. When a patient directs records into a consumer app that isn't a covered entity or business associate, that copy leaves HIPAA. What remains is the app's privacy policy, the FTC Act and Health Breach Notification Rule, and state law — a thinner and less predictable backstop.

The rule is final but not yet in force (effective ~January 2027; compliance ~July 2027). That gives providers, app developers and patient advocates time to make the hand-off safer: clear warnings, plain-language app policies, and easy deletion.

In this week's LifeeCode brief I also cover a JAMA Network Open study of AI-drafted NHS discharge summaries, emotional-support chatbot use in Japan, a clinic group's SEC breach filing, and wearables and exercise guidelines.
