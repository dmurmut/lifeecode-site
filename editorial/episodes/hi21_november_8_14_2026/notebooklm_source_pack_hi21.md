## Research Summary for NotebookLM

**Timeline.** DPDP Rules, 2025 were published in the Gazette on 13 November 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E)). Rule 1 phases them in: Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 (setting up the Data Protection Board) on publication; Rule 4 (Consent Managers) one year after publication, i.e. 13 November 2026; Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23 eighteen months after publication, i.e. around 13 May 2027. The May 2027 group includes notice (Rule 3), security safeguards, breach intimation (Rule 7), children's data (Rule 10) and the rights machinery. [H1]

**What a Consent Manager is.** The Act (s.2(g)) defines a Consent Manager as a person registered with the Board who acts as a single point of contact for a person to give, manage, review and withdraw consent through an accessible, transparent and interoperable platform. Under s.6(7)–(9), the Consent Manager is accountable to the person and acts on her behalf. [H2]

**What a Consent Manager must do.** The Rules' First Schedule: company incorporated in India; adequate technical, operational and financial capacity; net worth of at least ₹2 crore; interoperable, independently certified platform (Part A). It must not be able to read the personal data it helps share; acts in a fiduciary capacity; keeps records for at least seven years; cannot subcontract; discloses shareholders above 2%; and needs Board approval for a transfer of control (Part B). [H1]

**What opened this week.** News reports quoting a MeitY release say that on 14 November 2026 the ministry announced that eligible Indian companies can now apply for registration as Consent Managers through the Board's portal, that the framework took effect on 13 November, and that the Board will publish a list of registered Consent Managers. MeitY Secretary S Krishnan launched the portal. No Consent Manager is registered yet. [H3][H4][H5]

**What starts in May 2027, not now.** Breach intimation: tell affected people without delay and the Board within seventy-two hours of becoming aware (Rule 7). Children (under 18): verifiable consent of the parent (Rule 10). Penalty ceilings in the Act's Schedule include up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, up to ₹200 crore for failing to report a breach, up to ₹200 crore for children's-data obligations, and up to ₹50 crore residual. These are maximums. [H1][H2]

**Health in the law.** The Act has no separate "sensitive health data" category. Health appears in legitimate-use grounds such as medical emergencies and epidemics/public-health threats (s.7(f)–(g)). Clinical establishments, mental-health establishments and healthcare professionals are exempt from the parental-consent and no-tracking rules only to the extent needed to provide health services to the child (Rule 12, Fourth Schedule). [H1][H2]

**ABDM is separate.** ABDM already runs a Health Information Exchange and Consent Manager (HIECM) gateway where health data is exchanged only after the patient's consent. No official source links it to DPDP Consent Managers. [H10]

**Why it matters — allegations.** A case was registered by the Telangana Cyber Security Bureau over an alleged leak of a Hyderabad hospital's patient data (reported 3 Aug 2026). The Supreme Court issued notice (6 Aug 2026) on a plea alleging that data of more than 1.5 lakh Indian citizens, including medical records, was transferred to a foreign server. Neither is a proven finding. [H8][H9]

## NotebookLM-Friendly Narrative Brief

जब कोई lab app blood report दिखाने से पहले आपके contacts और location माँगता है, तो ज़्यादातर लोग जल्दी में "Allow all" दबा देते हैं। यही consent की असली समस्या है — consent एक button बन गई है। अच्छी consent के तीन हिस्से हैं: company पूछे कि कौन सा data चाहिए, बताए कि किस काम के लिए, और आपको उसे वापस लेने का आसान रास्ता दे।

India का DPDP law इसी दिशा में एक ढाँचा बना रहा है, लेकिन हिस्सों में। नवंबर 2025 में notify हुए Rules में से Consent Manager वाला हिस्सा 13 नवंबर 2026 को लागू हुआ। Consent Manager एक registered platform होगा, जहाँ से आप अपनी consent एक जगह से दे, देख और वापस ले सकें। वह आपकी तरफ़ से काम करेगा, आपके data को पढ़ नहीं सकेगा, और उसे कड़ी शर्तें माननी होंगी। अभी registration सिर्फ़ खुला है — कोई Consent Manager अभी काम नहीं कर रहा।

असली सुरक्षाएँ मई 2027 में आएँगी: breach होने पर लोगों को बताना और Board को 72 घंटों में सूचना देना, बच्चों के data के लिए parent की consent, और बाकी duties। जुर्माने की सीमाएँ बड़ी हैं, पर वे ऊपरी सीमाएँ हैं और तभी असर में आएँगी। Law health data को अलग "sensitive" category नहीं मानता, और ABDM का अपना consent system इससे अलग है। तब तक, सबसे काम की चीज़ है हमारी अपनी आदतें।

## Suggested Practical Advice Section

1. Consent के तीन सवाल: **क्या data ले रहे हैं? किस काम के लिए? वापस कैसे लूँ?**
2. Health या lab app में "Allow all" से पहले permissions देखें — report देखने के लिए contacts या location ज़रूरी नहीं।
3. Lab reports family WhatsApp group में forward करने से पहले सोचें — वह data अब कई phones में है।
4. ABHA से जुड़ी consent requests ध्यान से देखें; जो समझ न आए, उसे approve न करें।
5. कोई app "government-approved Consent Manager" होने का दावा करे, तो Board की official list आने तक सावधान रहें।
6. मई 2027 याद रखें — उसके बाद companies पर breach बताने और आपके rights मानने की कानूनी ज़िम्मेदारी लागू होगी।
