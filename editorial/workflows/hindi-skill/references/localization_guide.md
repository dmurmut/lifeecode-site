# India Localization Guide for LifeeCode Hindi

Use this guide when a topic is scientifically valid but risks sounding like a generic global explainer.

## Core Principle

Do not localize by translation alone. Localize by **listener reality**.

A good LifeeCode Hindi episode should make an Indian listener think:

> "Haan, yeh meri ya mere aas-paas ke logon ki life jaisa lag raha hai."

## The Three Layers of Localization

| Layer | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Evidence layer** | The best available science or policy source | Sleep-tracker validation study, privacy review, clinician guidance |
| **Market layer** | Indian adoption, consumer behavior, pricing, or familiarity | Budget smartwatches, online sales, gift purchases, mass-market wearables |
| **Lifestyle layer** | Everyday lived reality that shapes interpretation | Commute stress, late dinners, shift work, exam pressure, family responsibilities |

A strong episode usually has **all three**.

## Safe Localization Pattern

Use this structure whenever the science is global but the audience is Indian:

1. Say why the topic matters in India.
2. Explain what evidence exists.
3. Clarify what the evidence does **not** prove.
4. Translate the implication into Indian use cases.

This keeps the episode honest and useful.

## Familiar Indian Listener Scenarios

Use these as prompts, not as mandatory phrases.

| Topic type | Useful Indian scenarios |
|---|---|
| **Sleep / wearables** | Long commute, late dinner, shift work, exam pressure, late-night scrolling, family interruption |
| **Fitness metrics** | Sale-purchase smartwatch, office wellness motivation, New Year fitness attempt, walking goals |
| **Privacy / apps** | Using default app permissions, comparing features not data practices, family-shared devices |
| **Telehealth / AI health tools** | Convenience-first use, language comfort, uncertainty about what is trustworthy |
| **Stress / recovery** | Work pressure, caregiving, urban pace, aspirational self-optimization |

## What to Avoid

Avoid these weak localization patterns:

| Avoid | Why it is weak |
|---|---|
| Mere translation of an English article | Sounds imported, not lived-in |
| Inventing India-specific scientific certainty | Misleading if evidence is global |
| Overloading with statistics but no listener story | Feels abstract |
| Sounding like a gadget reviewer | Misses the LifeeCode interpretation angle |

## Good Framing Questions

Before finalizing an episode, ask:

1. **Why would an Indian listener care right now?**
2. **What ordinary behavior makes this topic familiar?**
3. **What confusion is the listener likely to have?**
4. **What is the most useful practical takeaway?**

If those answers are weak, the episode is not localized enough yet.
